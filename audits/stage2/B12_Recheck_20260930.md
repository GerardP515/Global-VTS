## 1. IJmuiden North and IJmuiden West Outer

The signed directive took effect on 30 September 2025 [SRC-062].
Its scope includes territorial approaches within 12 nautical miles of the breakwater ends, plus the extended IJ-geul.
Its page 10 map was inspected. It distinguishes the coastal sector from the supporting offshore radio channel.
It does not supply an offshore coordinate polygon or label either TSS.

Page 8 requires inbound masters to report to **IJmuiden Approaches on VHF 7** when contact becomes possible.
The same section describes an information-only service on that channel.
Page 6 separately requires seaward arrival reporting at 12 nautical miles on **VHF 61**.

These facts resolve the existence of an approach-reporting obligation. They do not resolve the offshore TSS service footprint.
A radio-contact trigger is not a geographical VTS boundary.
The inclusion of the extended IJ-geul cannot automatically be extended to adjacent TSS traffic lanes.
The southern tip of IJmuiden North is close to the nominal coastal radius; it is not labelled wholly outside.

The approved project rule includes TSS monitoring from VTS centres outside statutory VTS limits.
No opened authority source established that monitoring relationship for these two named schemes.
Therefore both retain **Unresolved**, with VTS-0025 retained as a candidate and the confirmed approach procedure recorded separately.

**To close:** obtain a current authority diagram with coordinates or a written, named-TSS statement of service/monitoring coverage.
This would preferably identify which IJmuiden North and West Outer lanes are served beyond the coastal radius.

### Off Texel: partial, not whole-scheme coverage

The source TSS extends well south and west of the VTS polygon [SRC-003, pp.111–112].
A section of its northern north-eastbound lane lies inside the declared VTS area.
An interior sample lies more than one kilometre inside the working VTS boundary.
Other lane interiors lie well outside.
Both source coordinate sets use WGS84, so no datum conversion is needed for this finding.

**Finding:** confirmed partial association with **VTS-0044**.
The source name “Off Texel” is not evidence that the entire identically named TSS is covered.

### Off Vlieland: partial coverage

The north/eastbound, westbound and south-westbound lane interiors overlap the VTS polygon [SRC-003, pp.113–114].
Eastern lane ends and part of the south-western end extend outside it.
The inside and outside comparisons remain unchanged under the datum sensitivity tests described below.

**Finding:** confirmed partial association with **VTS-0044**.
Exact boundary-crossing positions remain a separate publication check because the IMO coordinates use European datum.

### Vlieland North: association confirmed; exact edge unresolved

Both lane interiors lie inside VTS Off Texel.
The north-west end is nearly coincident with the published VTS edge.
The working ED50 conversion places one corner approximately 30–35 metres outside a straight-line boundary.
A local correction derived from adjoining source coordinates produces the same practical result.

This difference is smaller than the resolution implied by the rounded VTS coordinates.
It is not evidence of a meaningful exclusion, but it prevents a precise whole-footprint assertion.

**Finding:** confirmed association with **VTS-0044**; both lane interiors are covered.
Exact whole-scheme coverage remains open pending a current WGS84 chart or authority confirmation.

## Coordinate method and limits

The original map and coordinate tables were visually checked.
Working lane polygons were assembled from the cited IMO boundary vertices, not from bounding rectangles or convex hulls.
The VTS polygon was transcribed independently from the Netherlands and UKHO notices.
The helper script and input coordinates are retained beside the evidence manifest.

Off Texel uses WGS84. The Vlieland description says **European datum**.
ED50 was tested as a working interpretation, not silently treated as an expressly named source datum.
A second check used matching endpoints printed in the adjoining WGS84 Off Texel and European-datum Vlieland descriptions.
Those correspondences imply approximately −0.05 minutes latitude and −0.08 minutes longitude locally.

The comparison uses lane interiors to establish robust overlap and exclusion.
No percentage of operational coverage is asserted from these illustrative polygons.
A 200-metre sensitivity margin was used to distinguish clear overlap from rounded or datum-dependent boundary edges.
That margin is an analytical test, not a legal buffer or navigational tolerance.

## Reporting-system screen

No separate IMO system for these five schemes appears in the retained Part I baseline.
The national/operator sources reviewed here describe ordinary VTS or approach-reporting requirements.
No new national MRS identifier is justified by those documents.

This is not a claim that Dutch waters have no statutory reporting requirements.
The current consolidated STZ text was not successfully retrieved in this pass.
The signed IJmuiden directive and official Off Texel operator notice provide the fresh operational-report evidence.
Their requirements remain recorded even where the TSS-to-service boundary is unresolved.
No inference of “no reports required” may be drawn from the MRS column.

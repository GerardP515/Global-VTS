## Pass 1: primary authority research

### United States

USCG sources confirm that VTS Los Angeles-Long Beach extends 25 nautical miles seaward from Point Fermin. The IMO Santa Barbara Channel scheme reaches the Los Angeles-Long Beach continuation at approximately 118°36'W. The eastern part of the Santa Barbara Channel TSS lies inside the 25 nm VTS area.

**TSS-0173:** existing VTS-0012, partial coverage, no new service.

### Mexico: Salina Cruz

The current ASIPONA Salina Cruz website republishes the port's operating rules as applicable rules on 27 November 2025 [SRC-2289].

Appendix XII defines the **Centro de Control de Tráfico Marítimo del Puerto de Salina Cruz** and the **Servicio de Tráfico Marítimo Portuario y Costero**. The service is H24. The rules state that direct CCTM-vessel communication applies when a vessel enters the traffic-separation scheme and make use of the maritime traffic service mandatory for applicable SOLAS vessels navigating in the CCTM service zone **or the traffic-separation scheme** to call at Salina Cruz. The rules say the service follows IMO resolution A.857(20).

A federal audit of the 2024 equipment programme provides current corroboration of the CCTM equipment project [SRC-2290].

This is sufficient to move TSS-0175 from a historical lead to a current positive TSS-linked operational traffic service.

**TSS-0175:** new core service VTS-0301.

### Panama

AMP's own 2025 material shows that a national maritime traffic-control system remained an acquisition/implementation project [SRC-2291]. The 2025-2026 institutional reporting continues to treat the national system as a project [SRC-2292]. No current AMP service area was found for the remote Morro de Puercos or Isla Jicarita TSS.

Accordingly, TSS-0177 and TSS-0178 cannot safely be converted to a negative finding, because the project standard requires authoritative absence evidence rather than an unsuccessful search.

The Panama Canal Authority evidence for TSS-0176 and TSS-0213 was already reworked in the Decision 7 audit. It supports one **supplemental Canal-wide Marine Traffic Control / VTMS identity**, VTS-0228, with Flamenco and Cristobal signal stations. The service relationship is kept as boundary-only and included by default because the current ACP sources support conflicting readings of the outer service extent.

### Peru

Official Peruvian material confirms VTS Callao and identifies Callao as the first Peruvian port with the VTS, with installation at other ports planned [SRC-2293]. Current 2026 official material for Paita and Chimbote records traffic-separation buoy work but does not state that a VTS has been commissioned [SRC-2294].

That material strengthens the case that Callao is the established service, but it does not prove that no qualifying service has since been commissioned at every other Peruvian port. Searches for current DICAPI/HIDRONAV material did not establish a VTS at Talara, Paita, Salaverry, Chimbote, Pisco, San Nicolas or Ilo.

Those seven records therefore remain **UNRESOLVED**, rather than being turned into negative findings.

The Callao relationship was already closed in the Decision 7 audit: VTS-0227 / TRAMAR, full TSS coverage, core service.

### Cuba

The current project evidence remains a recent NGA description of Cuban TSS being observed by control posts, not a Cuban VTS designation. A historical Cuban state-media source confirms a radiotechnical border post at Cabo San Antonio but does not establish a VTS [SRC-2295].

No current Cuban national authority source defining a VTS or its area for the seven Cuban TSS was located. The control posts therefore remain leads only.

All seven Cuban records remain **UNRESOLVED**.

## Pass 2: source reopening

Positive findings were reopened against current or current-published authority material:

- Los Angeles-Long Beach: USCG / current US VTS material already reopened in Decision 7.
- Salina Cruz: current ASIPONA rules page and rules PDF [SRC-2289]; federal audit [SRC-2290].
- Callao: official Peruvian VTS material and Tier 1 TRAMAR rules, already reopened in Decision 7.

For unresolved groups, the current authority material was checked for evidence capable of closing the result. Where it did not establish either presence or authoritative absence, the record remains unresolved.

## Pass 3: boundary and applicability audit

- **TSS-0173:** partial physical overlap with VTS Los Angeles-Long Beach is established.
- **TSS-0175:** no geometric inference is required. The current-published Salina Cruz rules expressly apply CCTM communications and service participation to vessels entering/navigating the traffic-separation scheme.
- **TSS-0176 / TSS-0213:** retained as boundary-only supplemental links under the existing Decision 7 conflict rule.
- **TSS-0183:** all three Callao TSS parts fall within the TRAMAR notice/control distance; retained as Full from Decision 7.

No boundary claim is created for any unresolved record.

## Pass 4: identity and duplicate audit

### VTS-0301 Salina Cruz

No existing VTS entity in the current register represents the Salina Cruz CCTM. It is distinct from:

- VTS-0038 Veracruz CCTM;
- VTS-0227 Callao;
- VTS-0228 Panama Canal traffic control;
- other Mexican or US services.

One service identity is created for the Salina Cruz CCTM. The CCTM, its H24 maritime traffic service and the Salina Cruz TSS relationship are not split into separate records.

### Existing identities

- TSS-0173 reuses VTS-0012.
- TSS-0183 reuses VTS-0227 already integrated by Decision 7.
- TSS-0176 and TSS-0213 share VTS-0228. Flamenco and Cristobal are not counted as two services.

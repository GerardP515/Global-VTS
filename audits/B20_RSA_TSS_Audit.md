**Review stage after this audit:** Identity checked; association screening started

## Batch rule reminder

- A nearby port control station is not a VTS covering the TSS unless a source establishes coverage of the scheme, part of it, or a directly relevant approach.
- UKMTO / IMSC / IFC-IOR / BMP security reporting is not an IMO SOLAS V/11 scheme and is not a VTS.
- 2026 operational disruption in the Strait of Hormuz does not delete the adopted TSS identity.
- A failed search is unresolved, not confirmed absent.

## TSS-0098 — Off Ra's al Kuh

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| Official name (working) | Off Ra’s al Kuh (NGA also: Damagheh-ye Kuh) |
| States | Iran |
| Recognition basis | IMO-adopted. COLREG.2/Circ.54 Annex 1 is the adopting instrument for the *new traffic separation scheme off Ra’s al Kuh*. Two 2-mile lanes, 2-mile separation zone, associated inshore zone. Reference chart BA 2851 (WGS 84). |
| Grouping note | Single named scheme. Keep the official apostrophe form from the circular. |
| VTS screening | **Not established as TSS coverage.** Bandar Abbas Port Control exists as a port radio/control service (VHF 13/16). That is not, on present evidence, a VTS area covering this TSS. |

| Proposed relationship | None active. Log as a research lead: Ras al Kuh is a reporting point for Iran-bound ships. |
| Guide-entry hint | Intermediate only if Bandar Abbas service is later shown to cover the TSS; otherwise TSS-only / possible omit. |

**Sources**

- COLREG.2/Circ.54, Annex 1, *New traffic separation scheme off Ra’s al Kuh*.
- NGA Pub. 172, Sectors 12 and 13: IMO TSS off Damagheh-ye Kuh; Iran-bound reporting to Bandar Abbas Port Control on passing Ras al Kuh.
- SRC-001 candidate label.

**Unresolved:** current Iranian VTS nomenclature (PMO / PGA); whether any VTS area polygon includes this TSS; ALRS Vol. 6 Iran pages.

## TSS-0099 — In the Strait of Hormuz

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| Official name (working) | In the Strait of Hormuz |
| States | Oman (TSS lies in Omani territorial waters); Iran is the opposite coastal State |
| Recognition basis | IMO-adopted. First generation 1968 / A.284(VIII) 1973 cluster. Two-mile lanes and two-mile separation zone. 1979 amendment added an inshore traffic zone on the Musandam side. NGA Pub. 172 confirms the IMO TSS east of the Musandam Peninsula and Omani ITZ restrictions (through traffic under 19.7 m / sailing vessels). |
| Current operational overlay (2026) | Separate from scheme identity. NAVAREA IX 208/26 and Oman NtM (Jun 2026): existing TSS reported unsafe because of mine risk; temporary north and south corridors; waiting area 26°16.17'N 055°46.52'E; Oman radio A4N on VHF 16. Iran has published alternative lanes and a “PGSA” control claim. These are crisis traffic-management measures. They are **not** recorded here as a confirmed standing VTS under IMO Res. A.1158(32). |
| VTS screening | **Unresolved as a formal VTS.** Coastal States are directing traffic in 2026, but no pre-crisis official VTS users’ guide for “Hormuz VTS” was found. Do not create VTS-0xx from PGSA or IRGC instructions. |
| Reporting screening | **No IMO V/11 scheme on the adopted-SRS lists used by this project.** Regional security reporting (UKMTO / IMSC / BMP) and 2026 coastal-state VHF instructions are not a distinct SOLAS V/11 scheme. |
| Proposed relationship | None active. |
| Guide-entry hint | High-complexity passage if the product proceeds. Would need both-direction diagrams, ITZ note, and a dated operational-status warning. Not a simple template case. |

**Sources**

- NGA Pub. 172, Sector 13: two IMO TSS in the sector (Musandam / Hormuz, and Tunb–Forur); Omani ITZ rule.
- Washington Institute / secondary legal commentary: 1968 IMO TSS; 1979 ITZ. Treat as supporting, not primary.
- NAVAREA IX 208/26 (23 Jun 2026); Oman NtM on temporary corridors.
- SRC-001 candidate label.

**Unresolved:** exact official heading and geometry in *Ships’ Routeing* 2025; whether Oman or Iran operates any standing VTS that names this TSS; cut-off date for recording the 2026 overlay.

## Implications for the scoping count

Batch 20 is a cluster of internationally important TSS with **weak or no evidenced associated VTS**. If that pattern holds after ALRS and national-authority checks, a TSS-led VTS guide either:

1. carries short “TSS; no associated VTS confirmed” entries for these chokepoints; or
2. omits them from the VTS product and leaves them to *Ships’ Routeing* / ALRS / security guidance.

That is a product-scope decision, not a licence to invent a Hormuz VTS.

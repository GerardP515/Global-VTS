# Worldwide VTS Guide

## Project description

### Purpose

Witherby Publishing Group is assessing the feasibility and likely extent of a practical worldwide guide to Vessel Traffic Services (VTS) and associated vessel reporting requirements.

The proposed publication would help bridge teams understand reporting actions when approaching, entering, transiting and leaving a covered area.

Information would be presented in passage order, with separate directional guidance where appropriate.

The guide should make four things immediately clear:

**Who to call, when to call, on which channel, and what information to report.**

### Background

The starting brief proposes using *Ships’ Routeing* as the baseline for a worldwide inventory of Traffic Separation Schemes (TSS).

Each scheme is then researched to identify:

- an associated operational VTS;
- an associated mandatory vessel reporting scheme;
- both; or
- neither.

The immediate task is a scoping study. It is not yet a commission to produce the finished worldwide guide.

### Objectives

1. Establish the worldwide TSS inventory.
2. Identify associated VTS and vessel reporting schemes.
3. Separate TSS, VTS areas, VTS centres and reporting schemes as distinct entities.
4. Remove duplicate counting where several TSS share one VTS.
5. Identify VTS or reporting areas outside the TSS-led baseline.
6. Estimate the number of proposed guide entries.
7. Estimate research, editorial, charting and maintenance workload.
8. Provide enough evidence for a decision on whether the concept is practical to take forward.

### Intended outcome

The scoping study should answer:

> How many individual guide entries would be required, how substantial would those entries be, and what work would be required to research, produce and maintain them?

The report should distinguish:

- confirmed operational areas;
- unresolved candidates;
- editorial groupings;
- directional diagrams;
- working estimates.

### Scope

The project covers:

- IMO-adopted and other relevant recognised TSS;
- operational VTS;
- mandatory and relevant voluntary vessel reporting schemes;
- VTS centres and sectors where needed to understand service structure;
- source evidence;
- relationships between routeing measures and operational services;
- publication workload.

The project does not yet include full publication drafting or publication-ready chart production.

### Core principle

A TSS-led search is the starting point, not proof of a complete worldwide VTS census.

The study therefore maintains two scopes:

1. **Core scope:** VTS and reporting arrangements associated with the TSS baseline.
2. **Additional coverage candidates:** VTS or reporting areas found outside that baseline.

This prevents the phrase “worldwide VTS guide” from hiding gaps created by a TSS-only method.

# Research Plan

## 1. Purpose

Build a source-backed inventory of worldwide Traffic Separation Schemes, Vessel Traffic Services and vessel reporting schemes.

The research must produce a defensible count of:

- recognised TSS;
- TSS with an associated VTS;
- TSS with an associated vessel reporting scheme;
- distinct operational VTS areas;
- distinct reporting schemes;
- proposed guide entries;
- likely directional diagrams or passage variants.

## 2. Stage 1: Establish the baseline

### Inputs

Primary starting material:

1. IMO *Ships’ Routeing*, current available edition.
2. UKHO Annual Summary of Notices to Mariners, including the current TSS list.
3. ADMIRALTY List of Radio Signals Volume 6.
4. ITU List IV and MARS where useful.
5. National maritime authority, coastguard and VTS authority sources.
6. Historical Witherby material, where supplied, as a lead source only.

### Tasks

- Record the exact edition and publication date.
- Extract each candidate routeing measure.
- Preserve the official source wording.
- Identify grouped entries.
- Record later amendments.
- Separate current, withdrawn, superseded and unresolved records.

### Output

A source-linked baseline inventory.

## 3. Stage 2: Normalise the TSS inventory

For each candidate:

- assign a permanent canonical ID;
- record the official name;
- record previous names where known;
- assign an editorial region;
- record State or States;
- record adoption instrument;
- record implementation date;
- record current status;
- identify whether the source entry contains more than one scheme.

Grouped source labels must not be counted automatically as one TSS.

## 4. Stage 3: Pilot the method

Process two sequential batches of five.

The pilot should include:

- simple TSS/VTS relationships;
- multi-authority cases;
- reporting-only cases;
- grouped TSS labels;
- a case where no VTS is found;
- a case with conflicting evidence.

After batch one:

- audit evidence;
- check classification;
- check duplicate controls;
- amend the method if required.

Repeat after batch two.

The pilot is for method testing. It must not be used to extrapolate the worldwide VTS total.

## 5. Stage 4: Full verification

Research proceeds in batches of five.

For each TSS:

1. Verify the TSS against an authoritative source.
2. Search for an associated VTS.
3. Search for an associated vessel reporting scheme.
4. Confirm that any relationship is geographical and operational, not merely nearby.
5. Identify sectors, centres and reporting points where required.
6. Record the source and exact locator.
7. Record any conflict or uncertainty.
8. Run the batch audit before closure.

### Evidence hierarchy

Priority order:

1. IMO instruments and publications.
2. National maritime administrations and coastguards.
3. Operating VTS authorities.
4. Official hydrographic publications and Notices to Mariners.
5. Official port or waterway authorities.
6. Historical or secondary material as leads only.

## 6. Stage 5: Reconcile distinct operational entities

The same VTS may cover:

- several TSS;
- several sectors;
- more than one reporting point;
- more than one direction of traffic.

These must not inflate the VTS count.

The reconciliation should answer separately:

- How many TSS exist?
- How many distinct VTS areas exist?
- How many distinct reporting schemes exist?
- How many VTS centres exist?
- How many proposed publication entries are needed?

## 7. Stage 6: Identify non-TSS coverage

Compare the TSS-led findings against:

- ADMIRALTY List of Radio Signals Volume 6;
- national VTS lists;
- ITU station information;
- national ship reporting lists;
- historical Witherby coverage.

Create a separate register of relevant VTS or reporting areas that do not arise from the TSS baseline.

## 8. Stage 7: Estimate publication workload

Select representative examples:

- simple;
- intermediate;
- complex.

For each, estimate:

- source research;
- authority checking;
- technical review;
- chart or diagram preparation;
- editing;
- update burden.

Do not assume every VTS requires the same number of pages.

## 9. Quality controls

Each research batch receives three checks:

### Evidence check

Does the cited source support the finding?

### Classification check

Are TSS, VTS, reporting scheme, sector and centre correctly distinguished?

### Duplicate check

Has a shared service been counted more than once?

### Absence rule

A failed search is not evidence that no VTS exists.

Use:

- Confirmed present
- Confirmed absent
- Unresolved

## 10. Final outputs

The scoping report should state:

- verified TSS total;
- TSS with VTS;
- TSS with mandatory reporting;
- TSS with both;
- confirmed no-association findings;
- unresolved findings;
- distinct VTS areas;
- distinct reporting schemes;
- additional non-TSS candidates;
- proposed guide-entry count;
- estimated publication extent;
- workload and maintenance assumptions.

Confirmed totals, unresolved candidates and editorial estimates must remain separate.
